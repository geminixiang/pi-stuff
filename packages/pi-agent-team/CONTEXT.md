# Agent Team Domain

## Terms

- **Principal** — an actor that can originate a message: a team member, the user, or the runtime.
- **Channel** — an ordered conversation with stable identity and access rules. A channel is public, direct, or group.
- **Audience** — the immutable snapshot of members authorized to observe an envelope when it is posted.
- **Envelope** — one immutable message containing its sender, channel, audience, body, and wake policy.
- **Observation** — an envelope made visible to one authorized member. Observation does not necessarily wake that member.
- **Wake policy** — whether delivery merely records an observation (`passive`) or also makes the recipient runnable (`interrupt`).
- **Public speech** — an envelope posted to the public channel. It is distinct from finishing work. Passive by default (`team_say`); a member may instead post an interrupting public **broadcast** (`team_broadcast`) — the one member-originated exception to invariant 4, for when several specific teammates must respond to the same thing right now without a DM to each.
- **Direct message** — a restricted envelope between one sender and one member. Addressed by member id, or by display name when it names exactly one member (see invariant 5).
- **Group message** — a restricted envelope visible only to the immutable membership of a named group channel.
- **Handoff** — a direct interrupt whose purpose is to transfer the next action to one member.
- **Claim** — an atomic ownership attempt and synchronization fence. The result arrives later as a runtime direct message. Resource names are team-visible. Creating a group requires already holding a claim on that exact channel id — the runtime enforces this, not just doctrine, so a duplicate can't be created faster than it can be argued about. A brand-new poll id needs someone to hold a claim on it before anyone can cast into it, but that claim only reserves the id against a second, unrelated poll of the same name — it does not require the claim holder specifically to cast the first vote; once claimed by anyone, any member may cast, since voting itself needs no arbitration (see communication quality 5).
- **Release** — returning a claimed resource. Explicit by the owner, or automatic when the owner finishes or errors.
- **Errored member** — a member whose turn failed. Terminal like `finished`, publicly announced by the runtime, never silently retried.
- **Blocked member** — a non-terminal member that explicitly called `team_block(reason)` because it needs requester input, approval, or a requester-only resource. It keeps queued mail, claims, and poll eligibility but is not schedulable and cannot be woken by teammates; a requester intervention saves an honest `userInterventions` count, delivers guidance, and returns it to `ready`.
- **Background run** — a process/session-local detached `TeamRuntime` owned by `TeamRunManager`. It has a stable run id, monotonic `stateChangeSeq`, at most 512 lightweight body-free events, a run-owned cancellation signal, and non-polling waiters; at most 64 runs are retained per extension instance. It is not a daemon and is cancelled when the parent session shuts down.
- **Flush wake** — a final wake delivering undelivered passive observations before the team settles, so no member settles with unread mail.
- **Poll** — an opaque `pollId` members cast votes on (`team_vote_cast`), tallied once and only once by the runtime (`team_vote_close`), never by a member. Open ballots, not secret: the full vote breakdown is team-visible, same transparency stance as claim resource names. `choice` is opaque and exact-match too — the runtime does not interpret it, so two spellings of the same candidate (e.g. a member id and their display-name-plus-id) are counted as distinct, unmerged options; a live run showed exactly this split a winning candidate's votes 2/1 without changing the outcome only by luck. This is why doctrine (k) asks members to vote using a member id, consistently, when electing someone. Casting is otherwise silent, so once every eligible member has cast, the runtime posts a `POLL_FULLY_CAST` notice (invariant 21) — a live run showed all four members vote and then `team_wait`, with nothing left to ever wake anyone and close it, stalling the whole team quiescent before the game even reached its second phase.
- **Quorum** — at a poll's close, the members counted as still able to vote: those who already cast, plus every non-terminal member. Blocked members remain eligible because they can resume; errored or finished non-voters drop out instead of being waited on forever.
- **Settlement** — why scheduling stopped. `completed` means every member finished; `quiescent` means no member is runnable while unfinished members remain (`no-runnable-members` if an ordinary waiting member remains, `errored-members-remain` if every non-finished member is terminally errored, `blocked-members-remain` if every non-terminal remainder explicitly blocked); `exhausted` means the turn budget ran out with runnable members remaining. Detached runs opt into parking rather than settling while blocked members can still be resumed.
- **Turn end** — `team_wait`, `team_block`, `team_finish`, and `team_claim` end a member's turn immediately: the adapter self-aborts the underlying session so a member cannot loop calling the same tool dozens of times within one turn and exhaust `actionTimeoutMs` on internal re-polling instead of team decisions.
- **Same-source wave** — a wave whose every member was made ready by the identical envelope (typically the user's initial broadcast or a member's `team_broadcast`, both reaching everyone at once). Woken sequentially instead of concurrently, so each member's turn observes every earlier member's already-applied claims, groups, and polls before acting — see invariant 20.
- **Opening wave** — the same-source wave, if any, formed by a _single_ initial post reaching every member at once (not an array of distinct per-member envelopes). Its members independently draft a first take before seeing any of each other's opening public speech — see invariant 22.
- **Independence** — "independent agent team" bundles three distinct guarantees that do not all hold to the same degree; naming them separately avoids one being silently assumed from another. **Session independence** — each member has its own `AgentSession` and conversation history (`PiTeamAgent`, unique `sessionId`s, invariant enforced at construction). Fully physics, fully provided. **Epistemic independence** — a member forms its own view without first being informed by a peer's conclusion. Physics provides this only for the opening wave's very first turn (invariant 22); after that, whether a member reasons independently or anchors on public speech it has already observed is culture (doctrine, and how the objective/prompt is framed), not something the runtime can enforce. **Filesystem independence** — members do _not_ have isolated workspaces: every member's `PiTeamAgent` shares the parent process's `cwd` and full tool access, so two members can physically write the same file at the same instant. `team_claim` arbitrates _logical_ ownership of a named resource by convention; it is not an OS-level lock, and nothing stops an uncooperative or unaware member from writing anyway. This is a known limitation of the model, not a bug — "independent" here should never be read as "sandboxed" or "worktree-isolated."

## Invariants

1. Member IDs, names, and peer-list positions carry no scheduling meaning.
2. An envelope's sender, channel, audience, body, mentions, and wake policy never change after posting.
3. Only authorized channel members receive plaintext observations.
4. Public speech is passive by default (`team_say`); it enters every member's observation queue but does not itself consume an agent turn. A say may _mention_ teammates (`to`): the envelope stays public and passive for everyone else, but each mentioned member is interrupted — one message both informs the team and hands the floor to its named next speakers, which is how a conversation flows without a follow-up DM restating it. A self-mention is dropped (the speaker is already awake); a mention of a terminal member bounces the whole say before it posts, since silently not waking them would leave the speaker awaiting a reply that can never come. Beyond mentions, the runtime's `MEMBER_ERRORED` alert, `POLL_CLOSED` result, and a member's own `team_broadcast` are the only public interrupts.
5. Direct messages and handoffs interrupt exactly their recipient. An undeliverable or invalid command bounces to its sender as a runtime notice; it never fails silently or fatally. A recipient may be named by member id or by display name; a name resolves only when it unambiguously names exactly one member — two members sharing a name, or a name matching none, bounces rather than guessing, since guessing risks delivering to the wrong person. Sending or handing off to yourself is also invalid and bounces the same way: `post()` excludes the sender from its own message's audience by design (so a message to the whole team doesn't echo back), which made a self-addressed direct message or handoff look like it went out — posted, recorded, shown in activity — while never actually enqueuing, waking, or bouncing. That silent black hole violated this invariant; it now bounces like any other undeliverable command.
6. Group messages interrupt exactly the other current group members.
7. A passive observation is delivered the next time its member wakes for an interrupt, or in a final flush wake before the team settles.
8. `finish` is control-plane state, never public speech. A member must explicitly speak before finishing if the objective requires a public answer.
9. A claim is the only accepted command in its turn. No claimant may act as owner before observing `CLAIM_ACQUIRED`. Only the owner may release a claim; all of a member's claims release automatically when it finishes or errors, announced as public passive speech. Creating a group atomically claims an unclaimed channel id in the same command; an id claimed by another member is rejected, and a claim the creator already holds is honored. Creation itself is the arbitration point — commands are processed sequentially, so a singleton coordination structure still can never be created twice by two members racing in the same wave: the first create wins the id, the loser bounces exactly as a lost claim. A brand-new poll id requires _someone_ to hold a claim on it before the first vote can land, but any member may cast that first vote once it's claimed — the claim guards the id against a second, unrelated poll of the same name, not who gets to vote first into a legitimately reserved one.
10. "Restricted" (invariant 3) means restricted from other _members_ — it was never a promise of secrecy from the human operator. The live tool card (`TeamChatView`) renders every restricted DM/group body in full for the operator, the same as it would for any other tool call, and that rendering is part of the tool call's own result/`details` for as long as the host persists tool calls — not merely an ephemeral live view. What restricted plaintext _is_ excluded from is `TeamResult`'s own durable, machine-consumable shape: `restrictedMessages` carries only audience/body hashes (invariant 11 extends the same redaction to audit `events`), the form a downstream program, redacted transcript, or non-operator reader would consume.
11. Audit events contain body hashes and audience hashes, never restricted plaintext.
12. Settlement never asserts that the user objective is correct.
13. Audit distinguishes `message.enqueued` (queued for a member) from `message.observed` (actually delivered at a wake); enqueueing never claims observation.
14. A member whose turn fails becomes `errored`: terminal, publicly announced, its claims released, and further messages to it bounce.
15. Members hold full pi-coding-agent capability plus the team tools; only extensions are withheld so a member cannot recursively start teams. Synchronous execution remains foreground-only. Detached TUI/RPC runs expose an explicit requester intervention channel; every accepted `team_prompt` is posted from the `user` principal and increments `userInterventions`, never disguised as runtime speech.
16. `team_wait`, `team_block`, `team_finish`, and `team_claim` end a member's turn: the adapter self-aborts its session immediately after queuing one, so no member can burn its turn budget on repeated same-turn tool calls when there is nothing new to observe. This protocol is owned by a single `TurnState` (`src/turn-state.ts`), a pure state machine with no session or I/O — every custom tool's `execute()` routes its command through the same `apply()`, so the invariant has exactly one place it can be enforced or drift. `TeamAgent` is itself a public interface, though, and not every implementation is guaranteed to route through `TurnState` — so the runtime independently re-enforces the same turn-ending physics on whatever command batch `act()` returns: a `claim` anywhere in the batch is applied alone (everything else discarded), and otherwise the batch is truncated at the first `wait`/`block`/`finish`, before any command in it is applied. A well-behaved `TurnState`-gated batch is already correctly shaped, so this is a no-op for `PiTeamAgent`; it only changes behavior for an agent that isn't gated the same way.
17. Quiescence distinguishes terminal failures (`errored-members-remain`), deliberate requester waits (`blocked-members-remain`), and ordinary deadlock (`no-runnable-members`). With `waitForIntervention`, a blocked run parks on a promise rather than busy-looping or settling; only intervention or cancellation resumes the scheduler.
18. A member's wake may be preceded by a random `reactionDelayMs` stagger (off by default; product surfaces opt in) so a burst of concurrent wave members doesn't act, or visually present as active, at the exact same instant.
19. A poll is tallied exactly once, by the runtime, never by a member: the first `team_vote_close` locks in the result permanently — later closes of the same poll bounce with the stored outcome instead of recomputing, and casting after close is rejected. Ties are reported honestly (`kind: "tie"`) and never broken automatically, consistent with invariant 12. Open (unclosed) poll tallies are live state in the digest; closed results are a public `POLL_CLOSED` announcement, not digest state — same lifecycle claims already have (released claims drop out of `digest.claims`).
20. A same-source wave (every member made ready by the identical envelope) is woken sequentially, in wave order, instead of concurrently — waves caused by distinct envelopes (directed messages, handoffs) still run concurrently up to `waveConcurrency`. This makes claim/group/poll contention within a broadcast-triggered wave resolve by turn order rather than by chance: the first member to act sees no prior claim; every later member in that same wave already observes it in their digest before deciding what to attempt, so N members told the same thing at once converge on one coordination structure instead of each independently creating their own. A wave promoted by the final flush (invariant 7) is never treated as same-source, since flush promotion shares no common cause envelope.
21. Casting a vote is otherwise silent: unlike a claim or a group, it posts nothing observable on its own, so an open poll can sit fully voted with nobody ever notified it's ready to close. The moment the set of cast votes covers every eligible member (invariant 19's `missing` reaching empty), the runtime posts one public `POLL_FULLY_CAST` interrupt naming the pollId and its voters — stated as a fact only, never as an instruction to close — waking everyone once, exactly once per poll. This is a liveness guarantee, not a correctness one: it only guarantees someone gets the chance to close, not that anyone will. `eligible`/`missing` are computed from terminal/deliverable state (invariant 19), which changes on a plain `finish` or `error`, not only a cast — so the last outstanding voter can close the gap to zero `missing` simply by finishing without ever casting. Every open poll is rechecked after any terminal transition, not only after a cast, or a poll whose last non-voter finished instead of voting could sit fully cast forever with the members who already voted never notified. A poll with zero votes is never announced fully cast just because every member eventually finished — `eligible` shrinking to nothing as members terminate would otherwise make `missing` vacuously empty with no one having voted at all.
22. An opening wave (a single initial post reaching every member at once) gives every member an independent first take before any of them see each other's opening public speech, even though same-source sequencing (invariant 20) still runs them one after another for claim/group/poll convergence. Public passive speech (`team_say`) posted by one opening-wave member while another opening-wave member has not yet taken its own first turn is held back from that recipient's mailbox instead of delivered immediately; direct messages, handoffs, `team_broadcast`, and a say's mentioned recipients are never held back, since those are the sender explicitly choosing to reach that recipient right now, not a passive conclusion that could anchor an undrafted first take. The instant a member's own first wake begins, its barrier lifts permanently. An explicit interrupt (direct, handoff, group, broadcast, or mention) reaching a still-undrafted member also lifts that member's barrier immediately: its held-back public context is merged into its mailbox in envelope-sequence order _before_ the interrupt itself, so a recipient never observes a later prompt before the public premise it refers to. Once every opening-wave member has drafted (or become terminal without the chance), everything held back for each of them is delivered and they are woken together, sharing one synthetic cause so the reveal itself sequences the same way any same-source wave does. Before this, same-source sequencing meant an earlier member's `team_say` would land in a later member's very first observation batch — that later member's "independent" first take was never actually independent, it was already informed by a peer's conclusion, which a same-source-woken team's own opening round can trivially reproduce.
23. A member id must be non-empty (not whitespace-only) and must not collide with the other two `PrincipalId` variants, `"user"` and `"runtime"` — every place that tells a real member apart from those sentinel principals (recipient resolution, digest state, bounce and audit messages addressed "to runtime") depends on that separation holding, and `TeamRuntime`'s constructor rejects the whole team at construction time otherwise. The extension additionally validates the raw member array _before_ it collapses into a `Map<MemberId, TeamAgent>`: a `Map` is inherently deduplicated by key, so two roster entries sharing an id would otherwise fold silently into one entry, quietly shrinking the caller's intended team with no error anywhere. `"all"` is reserved too, but only by the extension — it is `startMemberId`'s "start everyone" sentinel, meaningless to `TeamRuntime` itself.
24. A `TeamRuntime` instance runs exactly once. `run()` records `team.started`, accumulates all mutable state (finished, errored, claims, polls, envelopes, events) for that one run, and closes every agent session in its `finally` block; a second call would resume scheduling over already-terminal members and already-disposed sessions, silently producing a second, semantically broken `team.started`/settlement pair appended to the same audit chain. A second `run()` call throws instead.
25. `maxTurns` bounds total turns actually consumed, not just how many waves are allowed to start: a wave is truncated to the remaining budget before it runs, whether that wave would otherwise execute sequentially (same-source) or concurrently in one `waveConcurrency` chunk, so a single oversized wave can never push `totalTurns` past `maxTurns` before the next check would have caught it. Members cut by the truncation stay `ready` for a later iteration, or are correctly reported as still-ready when the budget is spent and settlement is `exhausted`.
26. A command batch is applied in order and fail-stop, never as a transaction. Commands that already committed are never rolled back, but the first rejected command halts the batch: everything after it is discarded rather than applied. Later commands in a batch were planned assuming the earlier ones landed — the trap this closes is a rejected send followed in the same batch by finish, which used to seal the member terminal before it could ever observe the bounce and correct course. The rejection bounces as COMMAND_FAILED (plus COMMAND_BATCH_HALTED naming how many commands were discarded), waking the member to replan with the structured error next turn. A member with a genuine cross-command dependency should still sequence it across turns (a claim fence, or waiting for a confirmation message) rather than assuming same-batch ordering provides atomicity.
27. Detached execution is an in-process control plane, not persistence. `team_start(detached: true)` returns before the run settles; `team_get` exposes only a bounded snapshot, `team_wait` uses a monotonic sequence and one-shot wake rather than polling, `team_prompt` is the sole requester-to-member intervention, and `team_cancel` aborts only that run. Event history is capped at 512 and strips activity bodies; final snapshots contain report/manifest/counts, never raw transcripts or audit logs. Parent session shutdown cancels every live detached run.

## Communication qualities

Team communication norms live in two layers. **Physics** is what the runtime enforces — behavior that is possible or impossible. **Culture** is what member doctrine (the system prompt) encourages — behavior that is expected but violable. A norm that can be physics should be physics: culture reloads from scratch every session and decays as context grows; physics does not.

Derived from effective human-team communication, an excellent agent team exhibits:

1. **Shared mental model** — every member, on waking, can see team state (who finished, held claims, open polls' live tally and missing voters, recent public speech) without reconstructing it from message history. _Physics: runtime-supplied state digest per wake._ The digest is only as good as what actually reaches the prompt: `TeamDigest` carried poll state from the start, but the adapter that formats it into the live turn text (`formatTurn`/`formatDigest` in `src/pi-agent.ts`) omitted it until fixed — a member could see `HELD CLAIMS` and `YOUR GROUPS` every wake but had to re-read past public speech to know a poll's live tally, an easy gap for a domain-model change to reintroduce since nothing but a test on `formatTurn` itself would catch it.
2. **Closed-loop communication** — sending is not delivering. Undeliverable messages (unknown recipient, finished recipient) bounce back to the sender as runtime notices, never fail silently or fatally. Important directed messages expect acknowledgment. _Physics: delivery bounces. Culture: acknowledgment._
3. **Audience discipline** — conversation happens in public by default: `team_say` with the expected next speakers mentioned in `to` both informs everyone and wakes exactly who must reply, so a public statement is never followed by a DM restating it. Directed messages are reserved for content that genuinely must not be public (per-recipient secrets, redactions) or exclusive next-action transfer. When truly everyone must act right now, `team_broadcast` says it once and wakes everyone. _Physics: channel kinds, mention wakes, and the broadcast interrupt exception to invariant 4. Culture: choosing correctly._
4. **Structured handoff** — a handoff is self-sufficient: situation, what is done, what the recipient must do, whom to notify after. The recipient never needs to excavate history to act. _Culture._
5. **Floor control without starvation** — claims arbitrate contention; ownership can end (release or auto-release on finish); no member waits forever on observations that will never wake it. _Physics: claim lifecycle and a final flush wake before quiescence._ The flush only rescues members with undelivered mail; a member who ends its turn having merely _promised_ a future action has nothing queued and nothing coming — a live 4-agent debate run went quiescent exactly this way when the moderator closed the debate with "please wait for the poll announcement" and then waited: the claim-is-a-solo-turn rule meant the poll id claim needed a future turn, self-mentions are dropped, and no event could ever wake her again while three teammates dutifully waited on her. The safe order is claim-first-announce-after, since `CLAIM_ACQUIRED`/`CLAIM_REJECTED` guarantees a wake-back. _Culture: doctrine (n) — never end a turn on a promise of your own future action._ Claim is for exclusive actions only — one valid doer. Actions every member is meant to do independently and redundantly (casting a vote, answering with your own yes/no) have no contention to arbitrate and should never be claimed; claiming them adds turns for zero synchronization value, confirmed empirically in a "Heads Up" run where every vote and every yes/no answer was preceded by a claim on a resource nobody else was ever contending for. _Culture: recognizing which actions are exclusive._ Choosing among options by majority is its own primitive, not a claim: `team_vote_cast`/`team_vote_close` tally in code, so a result can never be self-declared or miscounted by whichever member is most invested in the outcome. _Physics: runtime-computed tally, honest ties, quorum-aware missing voters, and a `POLL_FULLY_CAST` wake once every eligible member has cast (invariant 21), so full participation is never a silent dead end._ Creating a singleton coordination structure (a group, a new poll) is itself exclusive — arbitration at the moment of creation strictly dominates the optimistic alternative (create independently, reconcile duplicates afterward) whenever contention is near-certain and reconciliation is costly, which a symmetric broadcast to every member guarantees: everyone receives the identical instruction at once and is equally likely to be first. For a group the arbitration is folded into the create itself: an unclaimed channel id is claimed atomically with the create (invariant 9), one turn instead of a claim round-trip followed by a create. That round-trip's gap was real exposure, not mere latency — a live werewolf run leaked its night coordination into the public channel exactly there, when a wolf woke inside the window between its partner's claim and the group's creation, saw the held claim but had no room yet, and improvised publicly. A new poll id still requires a prior claim: the first vote must never silently spin up a poll under a mistyped id, so the explicit claim stays as the typo guard. _Physics: group creation acquires the claim atomically, poll opening requires holding one, AND same-source waves execute sequentially (invariant 20) so the contention that arbitration defends against is far rarer to begin with — the first member to reach a decision point has already committed it by the time later members in the same wave are even asked._ This still can't merge two _different_ ids chosen for the same purpose — that needs a shared naming convention (culture), not arbitration (physics); repeated live runs show naming convergence remains the harder, not-fully-solved half of this quality.
6. **Explicit decisions** — a decision must be spoken publicly to exist; `finish` is control state, never an announcement (invariant 8). _Physics._
7. **Honest escalation** — blocked, rejected, or uncertain members report immediately by directed message instead of waiting silently; finish summaries claim only what the transcript can support. _Culture._
8. **Closure discipline** — the last step of a chain notifies its initiator before finishing; a coordinator confirms all members before finishing; no orphaned waiters. _Culture, backstopped by the physics of the final flush wake._
9. **Signal over noise** — speak only to change someone's next action; otherwise wait. _Physics: public speech never interrupts. Culture: WAIT when idle._ This includes reminders: a directed "please continue" is only signal if the recipient would not otherwise wake on their own pending observations; when several teammates independently decide to send that same reminder at once — a pattern a live "Heads Up" run showed happening after every hint round, three separate members each nudging the guesser to ask the next question — it costs turns without changing the outcome. Not yet backed by physics: doing so safely would require the runtime to expose _that_ a directed message was sent to a given recipient without exposing its content (private sends aren't visible to bystanders the way claim/poll/group state is), which is more surface than the problem's severity (wasted turns, not a wrong or stuck outcome) currently justifies — reassess if doctrine alone doesn't bring the rate down. _Culture only, for now._
10. **Content-scoped secrecy** — audience discipline (invariant 3) is a physics guarantee about _who_ can observe a message, not about _what_ it may say about them; a correctly-addressed message can still leak a recipient's own hidden state to them. _Culture only — physics has no way to inspect message content._ Surfaced by a "Who Am I?" run where a copy-paste broadcast summary, sent unredacted to each of its recipients, told all four members their own secret identity before they guessed.

## Member doctrine

The culture layer above, phrased for the member system prompt (kept verbatim in sync with `src/pi-agent.ts`'s `systemPrompt`):

> (a) Converse in public by default: discussion, questions, conclusions, decisions, and results all belong in team_say, with the teammates you expect to reply listed in "to". Never follow a public message with a direct message restating it — one say-with-mention does both jobs. (b) Use a direct message or handoff only when the content must not be public (per-recipient secrets, redactions) or you are transferring exclusive ownership of the next action; make it self-sufficient: situation, what is done, what they must do, whom to notify after. (c) Briefly acknowledge a received handoff before acting on it. (d) If you are the last step of a chain, notify the initiator or coordinator by direct message before finishing. (e) When blocked, rejected, or uncertain, escalate by direct message immediately; never wait silently. If your next step genuinely requires input, approval, or a resource only the requester can provide — and no teammate can unblock you — call team_block with the reason instead of silently waiting or pinging teammates; you will resume when the requester intervenes. (f) Claim only before an exclusive action — one where only one member may validly do it (assigning a role, picking who goes first, modifying a shared resource such as a file, opening a new poll id); release it when done. Creating a group needs no separate claim — team_group_create claims its channelId atomically, so when your next step is a private room, create it in this turn rather than claiming and waiting. Do not claim before an action every member is meant to do independently and redundantly, such as casting your own vote or answering with your own yes/no/unsure — those need no arbitration, just do them. (g) If nothing you could say would change anyone's next action, call team_wait. (h) A finish summary states only what you actually did; if the objective requires an answer or public statement, call team_say before team_finish. (i) Never state a fact that is a specific member's own secret or hidden assignment in any message addressed to that member, including a broadcast summary sent to multiple recipients — redact or omit each recipient's own entry before sending to them. (j) When the team must choose among options by majority, such as electing a role, use team_vote_cast and team_vote_close instead of announcing votes and tallies yourselves — never count votes or declare a winner by hand. (k) Derive shared ids (poll and group names) from the objective in a fixed, guessable form rather than inventing your own, so independent proposals are likely to collide on the same claim and get arbitrated by the runtime instead of silently coexisting as duplicates. This applies to vote choices too: when a poll elects a member, vote using their member id, consistently — the tally is an exact string match, so "id" and "display name (id)" count as different options and can silently split what should have been one candidate's total. (l) When several specific teammates must respond to the same thing right now, mention them all in one team_say — or team_broadcast if truly everyone must act — never team_say followed by a team_dm to each of them. (m) Before messaging someone just to tell them to continue or act next, consider whether they will already be woken automatically by their own pending observations — if so, stay silent; only send that reminder if you have a concrete reason to think they are stuck (for example, you already waited a full turn and nothing changed). Several teammates independently deciding to send the same reminder at once produces duplicate messages that cost turns without changing the outcome. (n) Never end your turn on a promise of your own future action. After team_wait, nothing wakes you except a teammate's message — self-mentions are dropped, and the runtime cannot know the team is waiting on you — so "I will create the poll, please wait" followed by team_wait strands the entire team. If your next step needs a solo-action turn (team_claim), claim NOW and make the announcement after CLAIM_ACQUIRED wakes you; if it doesn't, just do it in this turn. (o) A group always includes its creator — never create a room on behalf of a team you don't belong to, or you become a member of it; ask a member of that team to create it instead.
